What Does the 1-Year Builder Warranty Cover in Texas?

BuilderWarrantyCheck · Texas Building Warranty Inspection

The 1-2-10 warranty decides who pays for repairs on your new Texas home. Here is exactly what each period covers — and what builders hope you never claim.

The 1-2-10 warranty, explained

Most Texas production builders provide a 1-2-10 warranty:

  • 1 year — workmanship and materials (paint, drywall, trim, doors, flooring, caulking)
  • 2 years — mechanical systems (electrical, plumbing, HVAC)
  • 10 years — major structural components (foundation, load-bearing framing)

The exact terms vary by builder — some offer a 2-10 structure instead — so always read your own warranty booklet. But the 1-year workmanship window is nearly universal, and it is the one most homeowners let slip by.

What's covered in year 1

Year-one coverage handles the defects that show up as a new house settles: nail pops, drywall cracks, sticking doors and windows, grout and caulking separation, paint blemishes, trim gaps, and flooring issues. These are cosmetic, but fixing all of them out of pocket can easily run into the thousands.

What's covered in years 1–2

Mechanical systems — your electrical panel and wiring, plumbing supply and drains, water heater, and HVAC equipment — are typically covered for two years. A defect like a failing AC compressor in month 18 should still be the builder's responsibility.

What's covered for 10 years

Major structural defects — foundation failure, load-bearing framing problems — carry the longest coverage. Note that "structural" has a specific legal meaning in your warranty documents; hairline stucco cracks usually don't qualify, but measurable foundation movement does.

What's usually NOT covered

  • Normal wear and tear and homeowner maintenance items
  • Damage from storms, floods, or other acts of nature
  • Appliances covered by manufacturer warranties (often separate)
  • Landscaping, unless it affects drainage against the foundation
  • Anything you modified or had someone else alter

How to file a warranty claim

  1. Document everything — photos, dates, and a written description of each defect.
  2. Submit in writing through your builder's warranty portal or email before the deadline.
  3. Keep copies of every submission and response.
  4. Follow up — warranty departments are busy; a polite paper trail keeps your claim moving.
